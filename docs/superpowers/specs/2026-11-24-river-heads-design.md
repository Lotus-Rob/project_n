# River heads: sources on high ground or in a pond, a band that narrows at its source

Status: **draft** (approach chosen by the user 2026-11-24; builder check done, its question answered by the user;
awaiting approval of this document).

## Intent

The user wants the map's rivers to rise somewhere believable (2026-11-24). Today all eleven start on open ground
(meadow, grassland, forest, sand), and the river's band ends there in a flat cut, so a river seems to appear from
nowhere. The user chose "Both": a river rises from high ground or from a small lake or pond, and its band tapers at its
source. Ponds are real water tiles in the world grid, not painted sprites (the user's pick, option A). The work is done
by hand-editing `assets/data/world.yaml` and teaching `gfx::DrawRivers` the source end (the user's pick, approach 1).
Rivers flowing out to the sea are fine as they are (the user, about the delta channel).

## History

- `world.yaml` is generated by `art/world_map.py` (flow accumulation: a tile is river once its gathered flow reaches
  `RIVER_SHARE`, so sources land wherever flow crosses that line), then hand-tuned; it is the source from then on, and
  a plain rerun of the script loses the hand edits. `--roads` rewrites only `roads:` and `fords:`.
- Rivers are game data: the file is hashed; rivers rasterise to `TileFlag::River` (`MarkLines`, `src/core/world_map.cpp`);
  bare river tiles are impassable, crossed only at fords and bridges. A longer river changes walls, paths, travel costs
  and the hash. Saves may break freely before 1.0 (project memory).
- `gfx::DrawRivers` (`src/gfx/river_draw.{hpp,cpp}`) shapes only a river's end: joined onto a river it meets
  (`core::JoinedEnd`, which treats a river's first point as its source), else on a tile into water ahead and fading
  (`Mouth`, `MOUTH_INTO_TILES`). The start is a flat cut. `gfx::AddBand` (`src/gfx/ribbon_band.{hpp,cpp}`) fades only
  the end (`Laid::fade`); the roads share it.
- The terrain spec's Outcome deferred giving rivers a flow order; nothing earlier decided sources, lakes or tapering.

## Goals

1. **Every river has a source.** Its first point is one of:
   - **high ground**: a hills, rock or snow tile (peaks are impassable anyway, but a river may rise beside them);
   - **a pond or lake's edge**: `shallows` or `deep_water` lies within three tiles straight behind it, back along the
     river's first segment (a pond the river flows out of, not one it merely passes);
   - **another river**: a branch (the delta, river 10), whose head lies on a river's line.
2. **Ponds are water tiles.** A new pond is 2 to 4 `shallows` tiles in the grid, drawn by the existing water wash,
   shore line and stamps; the river leaves from its edge. No new kind, sprite or format.
3. **The band narrows at a high-ground source.** Both bands (edge and core) narrow from full width to nothing over the
   first stretch of the river, so it reads as a stream thinning out up the hill rather than a cut.
4. **A pond-fed river runs out of the water.** The mirror of a mouth: the band starts a tile back inside the pond and
   fades in from nothing to full over that tile, so it melts out of the water's wash, the same look a river has where it
   meets the sea.
5. **A branch's head is left as it is.** It lies on its parent's band, which hides the cut; no taper, no fade.
6. **The rule stays true.** A shipped-data test fails if any river's first point breaks goal 1.
7. **Roads and fords follow.** `pixi run python art/world_map.py --roads` reroutes them over the new rivers and ponds;
   fords stay on river tiles.

## Non-goals

- Rivers widening downstream, or a flow order between rivers (still deferred).
- Teaching `art/world_map.py`'s generator the source rule: `world.yaml` is hand-maintained now, and a rerun of the
  generator already loses hand edits.
- Spring or waterfall sprites, or any new art.
- Moving river mouths, or any other river's course beyond its new upstream stretch.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- A pond or a longer river that walls off a road or a city, or reroutes travel noticeably: an upstream stretch stays
  short (about 2 to 7 tiles) and clear of the roads' corridors where it can.
- A pond that reads as a puddle of sea: it sits inland, clear of the coast.
- A taper that makes a short river vanish or look like a dashed line.

## Approaches

1. **Hand-edit `world.yaml`, taper in the renderer, guard with a test** (chosen by the user). Move each source by hand
   onto hills or beside a new pond; `DrawRivers` narrows a high-ground source and fades a pond source in; a shipped-data
   test keeps the rule. Eleven small edits, each checked by eye, matching how the map is maintained already.
2. **A source pass in `art/world_map.py`**: walk each river uphill or add a pond automatically. Repeatable, but the
   generator's height field no longer matches the hand-tuned grid, so it would guess slope from terrain kinds: more code
   than eleven edits, and likely still hand-fixed. Rejected.
3. **A `source: spring | pond` field per river**, the game making the pond tiles on load. A shorter file, but a bigger
   format and loader, and ponds missing from the grid you edit. Rejected.

## Design

### Data (`assets/data/world.yaml`)

- Per the recon table: prepend upstream points to rivers 0, 1, 3, 5 and 8 (and 6 and 7, or give them ponds) so each
  first point lands on hills, rock or snow; give river 9 (and 6 or 7 if chosen) a pond of 2 to 4 `shallows` tiles
  beside its first point. Rivers 2, 4 and 10 keep their points.
- Then `pixi run python art/world_map.py --roads`, and commit the rerouted `roads:` and `fords:` with the edit.

### Drawing (`src/gfx/river_draw.cpp`, `src/gfx/ribbon_band.{hpp,cpp}`)

- One classifier decides how a river starts, and both `DrawRivers` and the shipped-data test call it, so the test
  guards exactly what is drawn. It is pure and needs no device (core, beside `core::JoinedEnd`, or gfx; the builder's
  call). In order:
  - **branch**: the first point lies on or within a tile of another river's line (as `core::JoinedEnd` judges an end);
    drawn as today;
  - **pond**: the mirror of `Mouth`: water lies within three tiles straight behind the first point, looking back along
    the first segment; the line starts `MOUTH_INTO_TILES` inside the water and fades in over that stretch;
  - **spring**: anything else; the line keeps its first point and tapers in.
- `gfx::Laid` gains the start's shaping beside the end's `fade`: a fade-in length and a taper length (world units, 0 for
  none). `AddBand` scales each point's half-width by its taper share (0 at the start, 1 past the taper length) and its
  colour by the fade share, as it does at the end. The roads pass zeros and draw as before.
- The taper's length is a look value: `world_look.yaml`'s `river:` gains `taper` (tiles), loaded with the rest of the
  river shape. Starting value 1.5 tiles (the builder's call, tuned by eye).

### Test (`tests/unit/data/world_map_tests.cpp` or `tests/unit/game/shipped_world_tests.cpp`)

- "every shipped river rises on high ground, out of water or from another river": each river's start, as the
  classifier above judges it, is a branch or a pond, or a spring whose first tile is hills, rock or snow. A pond must
  therefore lie behind its river's first segment, not merely beside its first point.
- `river_draw_tests.cpp` gains cases for the three starts: a lone river on open ground tapers to zero width at its
  first point; one starting beside water starts inside it and fades in; a branch starting on another river is unchanged.

### What else moves

- Reference pictures that show rivers (`pixi run project_n reference --update`, after looking at them).
- The shipped-world tests (`tests/unit/game/shipped_world_tests.cpp`, `tests/unit/data/world_map_tests.cpp`). The
  user's rule (2026-11-24, option A):
  - **Pinned costs are snapshots of the map, updated with it**: `MORTAL_TO_OASIS` (173), `STRONG_TO_OASIS` (152),
    `nearest` (126), `sectNearest` (162), `farSide` (562) and the like take their new values, old and new recorded in
    the Outcome.
  - **Moved first, then the user asked**: when a city-to-city cost moves by more than about 5%, the offending river's
    new stretch or pond is moved and the check rerun. Only if no placement keeps it does work stop for the user.
  - **Stop and ask the user, never loosen**: a city or danger site no longer reachable from the capital; one of
    "three links fork around a danger"'s forks lost (jade_cave, bone_forest, ghostlight_woods, the way round longer than
    the way through) or its list of dangers on roads changed; the traffic bounds broken (2,500 to 3,300 on the roads,
    at least 2,000 in the busiest view).
- Docs: `river_draw.hpp`'s `DrawRivers` comment (the start), `ribbon_band.hpp`, `world_look.yaml`'s header (`taper`),
  `assets/AGENTS.md` (the source rule for anyone editing rivers), `art/AGENTS.md` (sources are hand-placed after
  generation).

## Left to the builder

- Each river's exact upstream points and each pond's tiles, within the goals and anti-goals; checked on the map
  (look-at-the-game) and against the roads.
- Rivers 6 and 7: a 6-tile extension or a pond, whichever reads better and disturbs the roads less.
- The taper's final length and the curve of its narrowing (linear or eased).

## Calls made for the builder check (listed for the user)

- One start classifier shared by the renderer and the test (branch, pond behind the first segment, spring), so the test
  checks what is drawn; ponds are placed behind their river's first segment.
- A pond-fed river fades in out of the water (the mirror of a mouth) rather than tapering; a branch is left untouched.
- The taper is a look value (`world_look.yaml` `river: taper`, starting at 1.5 tiles), not engine code.
- The source rule is a shipped-data test, not a load-time rejection: it is a content rule for this map, not a format
  rule.
- The generator (`art/world_map.py`) is not taught the rule.

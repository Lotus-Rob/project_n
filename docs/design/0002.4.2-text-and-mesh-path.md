0002.4 §5 and §6 say what chunk 3 shows. This document settles how, and makes three simplifications (§1, decisions 1
to 3 of §12). Where it departs from 0002 or 0002.4, §11 says so; they're updated with the code.

## 1. What chunk 3 builds

- **The mesh path** (§2): textured, coloured triangles the main thread writes each frame, drawn in the world pass after
  the sprites. Text is its only user in this phase.
- **A glyph cache on a custom `TTF_TextEngine`** (§3): SDL3_ttf shapes and lays out; `gfx` rasterises the glyphs it
  hasn't seen into R8 pages and uploads them in the frame's own copy pass.
- **Text items** (§4): the logic puts `core::TextItem`s in the snapshot; the main thread lays them out (cached) and
  draws them through the mesh path. The arena shows the name label and the vertical caption (0002.4 §2).
- **The strings table** (§5): `assets/data/strings.yaml`, English and Simplified Chinese, and a language setting.
- **Events and damage numbers** (§6): `core::Event` gains its first kind; the crystal's aura emits one per hit and App
  plays each as a floating number.

**Simpler than 0002.4 proposed** (decisions 1 to 3):

| 0002.4 | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A digit strip texture per font and size; numbers as sprite instances on it; `core::Batch::texture` and the sort key's texture bits to bind it (§4, §6) | Numbers are text: their digits come from the glyph cache and draw through the mesh path, like every other glyph | One path for every glyph instead of two. The strip was the only second texture a batch would name, so `Batch::texture` waits for its first real user (phase 7's meshes) and the `draw_list.hpp` shortcut stays. A number is two to six quads either way; hundreds a frame are a few thousand vertices. |
| World text sorted with the world by its foot, overhead text over it (§5) | All game text in one overhead layer, after the world | Sorting text into the world means interleaving mesh draws with the instance batches. Nothing in this phase needs it: the label and the numbers float over things anyway, and the caption only stands near the tablet. |
| (unsaid) | Game text keeps its size on screen and is anchored to a point in the world | Rasterised once at its pixel size, it stays sharp at every zoom (0.25 to 2); nameplates and damage numbers behave this way in most games. Text that scales with the world comes when something needs it. |

## 2. The mesh path

- **The vertex:** position (`f32` × 2, in target pixels), UV (`unorm16` × 2 on its page), premultiplied colour (RGBA8,
  red in the low byte): 16 bytes. Positions are in pixels because the main thread places every glyph each frame
  anyway (§4); a vertex never needs the world transform.
- **Buffers:** one vertex buffer of `MAX_MESH_VERTICES` (65,536: 16,384 glyph quads, 1 MiB), written each frame through
  a cycled transfer buffer in the frame's copy pass, beside the instances; and a static `u16` index buffer for quads
  (0, 1, 2, 2, 1, 3 per quad), built once. Quads past the limit aren't drawn, and are reported as instances are
  (`overflows`).
- **Draws:** a `gfx::internal::MeshRenderer` (`features/meshes.{hpp,cpp}`), shaped like `SpriteRenderer`: `Upload`
  in the copy pass, `Draw` in the world pass, one draw per run of quads on one glyph page. One pipeline: premultiplied
  over, as `Pipeline::Normal`.
- **Shaders:** `mesh.vert.hlsl` (pixels to clip with a `float4` transform, as the composite's) and `mesh.frag.hlsl`
  (the page's R8 coverage times the vertex colour). R8 only: an RGBA page (colour emoji, phase 7's meshes) adds a second
  fragment shader then.

## 3. The glyph cache and the text engine

- **`gfx::internal::GlyphCache`** (`resources/glyph_cache.{hpp,cpp}`): R8 pages of 2048², a new page when the last is
  full; each glyph by (font, glyph index) packed on shelves of its height rounded up to 4 pixels, with a pixel of
  padding. The packing is a pure class (`ShelfPacker`), testable without a GPU.
- **The engine:** a `TTF_TextEngine` whose `CreateText` reads the laid-out text's copy operations (glyph font, glyph
  index, destination rectangle) and asks the cache for each glyph; a glyph it hasn't seen is rasterised on the CPU with
  `TTF_GetGlyphImageForIndex` and queued. `DestroyText` does nothing: the cache outlives every text.
- **Uploads:** the queued glyphs go up in the next frame's copy pass, through one transfer buffer per frame, before the
  world pass draws them; a glyph whose page is new is drawn from the next frame on, never half-uploaded.
- **A missing glyph** (a character neither the font nor its fallbacks have) draws nothing and is logged once per
  character, at warning level.

## 4. Text items and layout

- **`core::TextItem`** (`core/frame.hpp`), in a new `Snapshot::texts` (refilled in place, as `instances`):
  - what: a `StringId` (§5), or a number (`i64`) with a `NumberStyle` (plain, signed);
  - where: an anchor at the previous and at this tick (world units, interpolated by alpha, as instances are), and an
    offset in pixels (the label sits above the head however far the camera zooms);
  - how: a `FontId` (`Label`, `Caption`, `Damage`: each a font file and a pixel size, a table in `gfx`), premultiplied
    colour, alignment (the anchor at the text's bottom centre or its top centre) and direction (`LeftToRight`,
    `TopToBottom`).
- **`gfx::Fonts`** (`gfx/text.hpp`, public, no SDL types) opens each `FontId`'s font once at start-up from
  `assets/fonts/` at its pixel size (shortcut: no display scale yet, so text is half as large on a 2× display; the scale comes with the settings screen, chunk 7); the Damage font has a second face with `TTF_SetFontOutline` for
  the dark rim, drawn first.
- **Layout is cached by content** (font, string or number, direction): a `TTF_Text` made once and kept while it's drawn;
  one not drawn for 300 frames is destroyed. Each frame the renderer places every item's cached quads at its anchor
  (interpolated, through the camera to pixels) plus its offset, and writes them to the mesh path.
- **`Renderer::Draw` and `Capture` take the frame's extra texts** beside the snapshot (App's floating numbers, §6), so a
  capture can show numbers as the game does.

## 5. Strings and the language

- **`assets/data/strings.yaml`:** one entry per string, by name, each with `en` and `zh` (Simplified Chinese):

  ```yaml
  cultivator_name:
    en: Cultivator
    zh: 修士
  ```

- **`core::StringId`** is an enum in `core/strings.hpp` with each ID's name, as `core::atlas::SpriteId` is for sprites,
  but written by hand: the code names the strings it uses, and the table must have every one in both languages.
  `data::ParseStrings` fails on a missing ID or language, a duplicate name or a string that isn't text, naming the line;
  an unknown name is a warning, as an unknown creature key is. It gets a seed in the `yaml` fuzz target.
- **The language** is a setting, `language: en | zh` in `settings.yaml` (default `en`); an unknown value is logged and
  English is used. Changing it in the game comes with the settings screen (chunk 7).
- **Loaded once by `main`** into `core::Strings` (the chosen language's text per ID) and handed to the renderer. Debug
  builds' hot reload stays creatures-only for now.
- The label "修士 Cultivator" is two strings shown as one line: `cultivator_name` in both languages, so the arena shows
  the mixed line in either. The caption is `tablet_caption` ("问道" in both).

## 6. Events and damage numbers

- **`core::Event`** gains `kind` (`EventKind::DamageNumber`, the first) and its payload: `position` (world units),
  `value` (`i32`). Events stay plain data in a vector; a second kind adds fields or a variant when it comes.
- **The simulation emits them:** `Simulation::Tick` gains `std::vector<Event>& events`, to which a tick appends; `Logic`
  stamps the tick and epoch and hands them over with the snapshot, as it already publishes its (empty) events. They're
  output only: nothing in the state changes, so saves and replays don't change.
- **The arena's aura** (0002.4 §2): once a second (every 60th simulation tick), each creature within 320 world units
  of the crystal's foot takes a hit of 5 to 40, drawn with `core::Random` keyed on the state counter and the creature's
  `StableId` (0002 §7.4); the event's position is the creature's head. Nothing dies.
- **App plays them** (`app/floating_numbers.{hpp,cpp}`): a played event starts a number that rises 60 pixels and fades
  out over a second of render-clock time, ease-out; each frame App turns the live ones into `TextItem`s (signed, the
  Damage font, red) for `Renderer::Draw`. At most 512 live: the oldest goes first. The `app.cpp` shortcut for the
  empty event player goes.

## 7. What the arena shows

The name label over the cultivator, the vertical caption beside the tablet (the `Stele` prop), and numbers floating
up from the foxes as they pass the crystal. The stress and still scenes show no text, so their reference pictures
don't move. A new reference picture, `text.png`, shows the label, the caption and three numbers at fixed values and
ages, on lavapipe.

## 9. Testing

- **The glyph cache:** `ShelfPacker` packs fake glyph sizes onto shelves, starts a page when one is full, and refuses a
  glyph larger than a page; a glyph is rasterised once however often it's asked for.
- **Layout:** one `TextItem` gives the same quads twice (the cache hit); a number's quads for 1,234 and -5 (digits,
  sign, separators); a missing glyph logged once.
- **Strings:** the table's parsing and each refusal (a missing ID, a missing language, a duplicate, not text); the
  language setting's default and an unknown value.
- **Events:** the arena emits a hit for a creature in reach, none for one out of reach, none between seconds, the same
  values on a rerun; events of an old epoch are never played (exists: PlayDue); a floating number's position and fade at
  a render tick, and the oldest dropped past 512.
- **Reference:** `text.png` (§7); the five existing pictures unchanged.

## 11. Departures

- **From 0002.4 §4, §6:** no digit strip and no `core::Batch::texture` (decision 1); text in one overhead layer
  (decision 2); text sized on screen (decision 3).
- **From 0002.4 §1, decision 1:** `phase6-text` merged after chunk 2 (PR #16, 2026-11-10), so chunk 3 is its own branch,

## 12. Decisions

The user's, 2026-11-10, all accepted:

1. **Damage numbers as text** through the glyph cache and the mesh path, with no digit strip and no
   `core::Batch::texture` this phase (§1).
2. **All game text in one overhead layer** after the world, not sorted into it by foot (§1). Accepted for now (2026-11-10), with care for later layered UIs: chunk 4's `ui` draws in the final
   pass over the composite (0002.4 §4), so the overhead layer must stay a world-pass layer the UI never needs to sit
   under or between.
3. **Game text keeps its size on screen**, anchored to a world point (§1, §4).
4. **String IDs as a hand-written enum** the table must cover in both languages, loaded once at start-up, without
   hot reload (§5).
5. **`Simulation::Tick` emits events** into a vector Logic stamps and publishes (§6).
